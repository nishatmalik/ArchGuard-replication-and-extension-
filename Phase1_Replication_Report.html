Improving LLM-Based Early Warning of Architectural Decay Using Prompt Engineering and Retrieval: A Replication and Extension of ArchGuard

Phase 1: Replication Report

Executive Summary

This study investigates early warning of architectural decay through the prediction of architectural smell-inducing (ASI) software issues.

The replication reconstructs the classification datasets and reproduces the supervised SVM baseline using the released OpenAI embeddings and detector-specific ground-truth labels.

The SVM results match the previously recorded published F1-score references at three-decimal reporting precision.

The released GPT-5-mini prediction cache exhibits zero positive-class recall and F1-score. This result characterizes the archived predictions and does not establish the performance of fresh GPT-5-mini inference.

1. Dataset Characteristics

The classification dataset contains 6,390 detector-specific records corresponding to 4,463 unique issues across three software projects.

There are 940 ASI and 5,450 non-ASI records. The overall ASI prevalence is 14.71%.

2. Experimental Methodology

  • Five project-detector configurations.
  • Two repetitions of five-fold stratified cross-validation.
  • Fifty SVM fold evaluations in total.
  • Released text-embedding-3-small embeddings.
  • RBF support vector machine.
  • Random undersampling restricted to training folds.
  • Positive-class precision, recall and F1-score.
  • Matched-record analysis for archived LLM comparisons.

The original embedding input identity and exact original fold identity remain independently unverified.

11. Proposed Research Extension

The proposed investigation evaluates whether reliability controls, prompt engineering, retrieval-augmented few-shot classification and structured architectural reasoning improve early ASI prediction.

The experiments must isolate the effects of these interventions using comparable held-out records.

Retrieved demonstrations must originate exclusively from the permitted training partition.

Prompt development must not use held-out test labels. Because Phase 1 involved exploratory analysis of the existing dataset, independent or temporal confirmation is desirable for a stronger final generalization claim.

15. Conclusion

Phase 1 establishes a numerically reproduced SVM baseline and documents limitations in the released LLM predictions.

The archived response failure patterns motivate a controlled investigation of reliable LLM inference and enhanced classification methods.

Whether these methods improve ASI prediction remains an open experimental question.
